How the services are grouped, and what to watch for
  • Departments are grouped by name. Each government names its departments differently, so they're sorted into common services.
  • Only the General Fund is counted. Some cities pay for a service partly from other funds (a roads fund, a fire assessment, the CRA), so their figure can understate the full cost.
  • Left out of every service: transfers, reserves, debt payments and hurricane-recovery lines. "Everyday General Fund" is the General Fund without those.
  • Contract services count too. Callaway and Mexico Beach pay the Sheriff for policing; that contract is their police cost.
  • Bay County is shown for context only; its figures are spread over every county resident. The school district isn't compared here.

Reading the comparison

  • Different jobs. The county serves everyone and runs countywide services; the school district runs schools; cities provide police, fire, water and other services that differ from city to city. Compare cities with cities.
  • Visitors and second homes count too. Panama City Beach and Mexico Beach serve far more people than live there, and much of their property is owned by non-residents, so their per-resident figures run high.
  • Residents are the State of Florida's 2025 population estimates. Budgets are the latest adopted budget on file; debt and audit findings are from the latest audited year. Each government's page links to every source.
  • Panama City Beach levies no city property tax; its revenue comes mostly from sales, tourist and utility taxes, fees and its CRA.
