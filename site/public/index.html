What's new

All updatesRSS
  1. Oct 23, 2026Savings finder: where governments could spend less A new page lists places Bay County's local governments could spend less, found in records already on the site: Front Beach Road segments still ahead after the finished ones ran over, CRA capital budgets left mostly unspent, engineering bought in many separate task orders, and services such as plate readers and paving bought separately. Each opportunity shows the projects or ledger rows it rests on, a low and high estimate with its stated assumption, the next question to ask and a ready-to-send records request. Estimates are illustrative, not findings that money was wasted.
  2. Oct 23, 2026CRA network: payees, connections and campaign gifts A new page shows every payee in the CRA ledger as a network: who each redevelopment agency paid, payees tied to each other by the same project, address or subcontract, and business campaign gifts to the officials who sit as each CRA's board. Four businesses both received money from a CRA record and gave to a campaign. A map and a ranked payee list use the same filters. Only businesses and PACs are shown as donors, and a gift and a payment together are facts about the records, not evidence of why either happened.
  3. Oct 23, 2026Pages now open on the current year Lists and tables with several years of records now open on the newest year that has records, never past the current fiscal year, with All years one tap away. That covers the Accountability console (and its headline numbers), CRA Spending, Contracts, How They Voted, Sales Tax Projects, the Hurricane Michael FEMA ledger, audit findings and other multi-year tables. A year with only a few stray records is skipped. Who Gets Paid now sorts by FY2026. Add ?year=all (or a year) to any page link to choose. Debt lists still show everything owed today.
  4. Oct 23, 2026Accountability console: projects from budget to close-out A new page follows public projects from the first budget to the day they close: approvals, contracts, change orders, the votes on them, payments and the final cost, each with its source. Questions are raised only by nine published rules (cost growth over 10%, running late, figures that don't agree, an unnamed payee and others), the same way for every government, and each is a question for the agency rather than a finding. Each project shows the records that would answer its questions, as ready-to-send records requests, and where the agency's reply stands. It starts with 18 project histories reviewed by hand, 37 contracts with change orders and 39 scheduled sales tax projects.

How this site works

Public records only

Figures come from audits, adopted budgets, state data, annual reports and council records. If a number isn't in a record, it isn't on the site.

Sources on every row

Each payment, project or finding links to the document it came from, so you can check it yourself. Download reports as PDF or data as CSV.

Gaps stated plainly

Where a government doesn't publish something, the page says so and names the record to request.
