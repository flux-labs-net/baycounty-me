What a line means

  1. A gray line is money from a government or agency. Thicker lines carry more dollars in the records. A contract award and the check register payments on it can describe the same money, so record types are never added together.
  2. A dashed line is a documented link between two payees: the same project, the same address, or one working as the other's subconsultant. Each one cites its record.
  3. A red line is a business campaign gift, and a dotted line joins an official to the board they sit on. A city commission also sits as its redevelopment agency's board. Only businesses, PACs and committees are shown, never individual donors, and none of this shows wrongdoing.

Business gifts to officials and candidates

Each row totals one business or group's gifts to one official's most recent campaign, or to a 2026 candidate, from Supervisor of Elections reports. Donors that were also paid by a local government are listed first. "Likely" means the names matched after a hand check rather than exactly. Gifts from individual people are counted on Campaign gifts, where people who run a registered business are named.

Sources and method

Payment records cover contracts, change orders, emergency purchases, check registers, tourism agency payments and CRA ledger records, from agendas, check registers, tourism agency payment files and each redevelopment agency's reports; every record links to its source. CRA ledger items that are only proposed or planned stay on the CRA ledger. Payees are matched to the site's vendor list by name. 602 payees are named; grant programs whose recipients aren't named are grouped, and private home addresses are left out. Campaign gifts come from officials' most recent reports and 2026 candidates' reports with the Supervisor of Elections; 29 businesses both gave to a campaign and were paid in these records. A gift and a payment appearing together is a fact about the records. It says nothing about why either happened, and most spending is approved by the full board.
