What's new

Updates

New pages, new data and corrections, newest first. Subscribe with any feed reader to get them as they happen: RSS feed.

BayCounty.me launched on Oct 20, 2026. The first entries below are the initial pages going live. From here on, each new dataset, data refresh and correction gets its own dated entry.

Change log

  1. Oct 24, 2026Business owners who gave are now named. People who run a registered business and gave to a local campaign are now listed by name, with the businesses they run and every campaign they gave to. A donor is listed only when the name and street address on the campaign filing are the same as an officer, manager or registered agent on the state business registry. Other individual donors are still counted only in totals, and addresses are never shown. Campaign Gifts · /people
  2. Oct 24, 2026Profiles for every name, linked everywhere. Every elected official, department leader and 2026 candidate now has a profile page with where they serve, campaign gifts from businesses and groups, recorded votes, votes on donors' contracts, pay and a map of their connections. Every other company, nonprofit, nonprofit board member, registered business and committee in the records has a profile too, with every documented connection and its source. Vendor and government pages gained the same connection map. Names of officials, companies and nonprofits are now links to their profiles across the site. Individual campaign donors are still never named, and officials' board seats and businesses appear only after an editor checks the match. /people · Connections · /connections/nonprofits · /public-money/vendors · Governments · API · Open Data
  3. Oct 24, 2026Connections: one network for every government. Campaign gifts and payee connections now have their own section, Connections, instead of sitting under the CRA pages. The network covers every government and agency with payment records: the county, the cities, the redevelopment agencies and the tourism agencies, across contracts, change orders, emergency purchases, check registers, tourism payments and the CRA ledger. It shows business gifts to every official who approves that spending, not only CRA board members, so 29 businesses that were paid and gave to a campaign now appear, up from 4. Campaign Money is now Campaign Gifts, and it and the Money map moved into the section; old links still work. Only businesses, PACs and committees are named as donors. Connections · Campaign Gifts · Money Map · CRA Spending
  4. Oct 23, 2026Savings finder: where governments could spend less. A new page lists places Bay County's local governments could spend less, found in records already on the site: Front Beach Road segments still ahead after the finished ones ran over, CRA capital budgets left mostly unspent, engineering bought in many separate task orders, and services such as plate readers and paving bought separately. Each opportunity shows the projects or ledger rows it rests on, a low and high estimate with its stated assumption, the next question to ask and a ready-to-send records request. Estimates are illustrative, not findings that money was wasted. /public-money/savings · /public-money/accountability · CRA Spending
  5. Oct 23, 2026CRA network: payees, connections and campaign gifts. A new page shows every payee in the CRA ledger as a network: who each redevelopment agency paid, payees tied to each other by the same project, address or subcontract, and business campaign gifts to the officials who sit as each CRA's board. Four businesses both received money from a CRA record and gave to a campaign. A map and a ranked payee list use the same filters. Only businesses and PACs are shown as donors, and a gift and a payment together are facts about the records, not evidence of why either happened. Connections · CRA Spending · Money Map
  6. Oct 23, 2026Pages now open on the current year. Lists and tables with several years of records now open on the newest year that has records, never past the current fiscal year, with All years one tap away. That covers the Accountability console (and its headline numbers), CRA Spending, Contracts, How They Voted, Sales Tax Projects, the Hurricane Michael FEMA ledger, audit findings and other multi-year tables. A year with only a few stray records is skipped. Who Gets Paid now sorts by FY2026. Add ?year=all (or a year) to any page link to choose. Debt lists still show everything owed today. /public-money/accountability · CRA Spending · Contracts · How They Voted · Sales Tax Projects · Hurricane Michael · Audit Findings · Who Gets Paid
  7. Oct 23, 2026Accountability console: projects from budget to close-out. A new page follows public projects from the first budget to the day they close: approvals, contracts, change orders, the votes on them, payments and the final cost, each with its source. Questions are raised only by nine published rules (cost growth over 10%, running late, figures that don't agree, an unnamed payee and others), the same way for every government, and each is a question for the agency rather than a finding. Each project shows the records that would answer its questions, as ready-to-send records requests, and where the agency's reply stands. It starts with 18 project histories reviewed by hand, 37 contracts with change orders and 39 scheduled sales tax projects. /public-money/accountability · Records Request · Open Data
  8. Oct 22, 2026A privacy page, and data for the iPhone app. A new privacy page says what the site and the BayCounty.me iPhone app collect: no accounts, no ads and no tracking. The site now also publishes the files the app reads, in /data/app: the tax and Amendment 3 calculators, the glossary, company and people profiles and the money map. They hold the same information the pages already show. /privacy · API
  9. Oct 21, 2026Company pages, and one map of public money. Every company with more than one record now has its own page: its contracts, change orders, payments, CRA money, gifts from the business to campaigns, and the votes that approved its contracts with how each member voted. A gift near a vote does not show why anyone voted the way they did. A new map shows CRA spending and 1,062 sales tax projects over CRA districts and city limits. Hurricane Michael projects are not on it because FEMA does not publish their locations. /public-money/vendors · /public-money/map · Contracts · Who Gets Paid · Money Map
  10. Oct 21, 2026New votes, FY2027 budgets, contracts and tourism payments. Added 30 votes (Panama City CRA and Commission, Callaway Commission and CRA) with each member's vote; the FY2027 budgets adopted in September for all nine governments; 17 contracts, mostly Springfield and Callaway; and tourism agency payments for 18 more months, each checked against the register's printed totals. Bay County Commission minutes after June 16 are not posted yet. How They Voted · Tax Dollars · Contracts · Who Gets Paid · CRA Spending
  11. Oct 21, 2026My Address: your officials, nearby spending, and privacy. My Address now links each local official to how they voted and to campaign money, lists CRA spending and sales tax projects near you, and links to your government's contracts, meetings and budget. To protect owners' privacy it no longer shows which kinds of exemptions a parcel gets (such as disability or veteran exemptions); it shows homestead and one total for all other exemptions. My Address · Exemptions
  12. Oct 21, 2026Share any view; alerts you choose; Spanish pages. Filters now stay in the page link, so you can share exactly what you are looking at. Search results open the record itself. You can now get an email when a contract over an amount you pick is approved, when a board member votes, or when an agenda mentions a word you choose, and change what you follow at any time. Tax Dollars, Fees and Bills, Exemptions, Elections and Meetings are now in Spanish. Email Alerts · Search · What's new
  13. Oct 21, 2026Contracts: how much each contract grew. Change orders are now added up for each contract and measured against its original amount, instead of listed one by one. Of the 22 contracts whose original amount is known, 12 grew by more than 10%, the point where Bay County and Panama City rules require board approval for the added cost. Each bar opens to its change orders, reasons and sources. Contracts whose original amount wasn't in the agendas are listed separately, and where an agenda states the new contract total, that total is used. Every individual change order is still listed below. Contracts
  14. Oct 21, 2026Contracts: one name per company, and company cards. Agendas, check registers and campaign reports spell the same company many ways, so the contract winners list was splitting some firms into two or more entries. The 322 contracts used 206 spellings; they now resolve to the companies behind them. Marshall Brothers, for example, had been listed at $11.1M and is now ranked sixth at $24.0M. A new Look up a company section shows everything on the site for one company: contracts by government next to what each government paid it (where a check register shows it), change orders, emergency purchases, campaign gifts from the company, and votes on its contracts by officials it gave to. Insurance pools owned by governments (Florida League of Cities, PGIT) are no longer counted as contractor wins. Contracts
  15. Oct 21, 2026Money map: full roll calls on donor contract votes. The table of votes on a donor's contract now shows each vote once, with every board member's vote, not only the members who received a gift from the business. Members who got a gift are listed first, with the amount and date. Most of these contracts passed unanimously on the consent agenda. One vote that had been left out was added: Panama City's June 24, 2025 award of the Millville plant generator bid to Marshall Brothers, which the minutes record under an unrelated plat item. Money Map
  16. Oct 21, 2026Sort, filter and download every list. Every table and list on the site can now be sorted by clicking a column heading, filtered with a search box (and drop-downs where a column has a few values), and downloaded as a CSV of exactly the rows on screen, in the order shown. Lists open newest first, then A to Z; upcoming meetings open soonest first. What's new
  17. Oct 21, 2026An API for BayCounty.me. Every dataset on the site is now available through a free, read-only API with filtering, sorting and CSV output, plus an MCP server so AI assistants can look up and cite these records directly. API
  18. Oct 21, 2026Correction: Money map. The first version of the Money map overstated connections. It counted a donor's contracts again for every official the donor gave to, which roughly doubled the government totals, and it drew lines from officials to governments they don't serve on. The map now puts each donor in the middle: campaign gifts on one side, the governments that pay the donor on the other, each counted once. It marks only gifts to an official whose own government pays that donor, and lists the recorded votes those officials cast on that donor's contracts. The heat map that credited contract dollars to individual officials was removed. Money Map
  19. Oct 21, 2026Who's running in 2026. Every local race on Bay County ballots this year, with each candidate's platform in their own words, links to their campaigns, and their campaign money: what they raised and spent, how much came from individuals, organizations and their own pockets, and every business or committee that gave. Races already decided are shown separately. 2026 Elections
  20. Oct 21, 2026Property tax exemptions, and exemptions on My Address. A new page shows every property tax exemption in the 2026 tax roll, from homestead to disabled veterans, with how many homes get each and how much tax it takes off bills. My Address now lists each exemption on a home by name and uses the certified school and non-school taxable values, which corrects estimates for fully exempt homes and for seniors with the county senior exemption. Exemptions · My Address
  21. Oct 21, 2026School Board votes, and more city votes. How They Voted now includes 104 School Board votes from January 2024 to September 2026 with each member's vote, including budgets, millage and major contracts; all 2024 Panama City Beach council votes, including the FY2025 budget; and Springfield votes from mid-2024 to October 2026, including both final budget hearings. How They Voted
  22. Oct 21, 2026Money map. An interactive map of which businesses and groups gave to which officials, and where those same donors hold contracts with a local government, plus heat maps of giving by government and of contract dollars held by each official's donors. Money Map
  23. Oct 21, 2026Budget explorer. Open any government's FY2026 budget: spending by fund, General Fund spending by department, and revenue by source. Includes Callaway's and Parker's adopted budgets, which weren't on the site before. Budget Explorer
  24. Oct 21, 2026Who gets paid: tourism agencies. Payments by Visit Panama City Beach, Destination Panama City and the Mexico Beach CDC, read from the Clerk of Court's check registers and checked against each month's printed total. Who Gets Paid
  25. Oct 21, 2026Every bond, note and loan. The Debt page now lists each of 61 bond, note and loan issues with the amount borrowed, what is still owed, the rate, the final payment date and what it paid for. Debt
  26. Oct 21, 2026A page for each government. Bay County, the School Board and each of the seven cities now have one page that pulls together who runs it, its budget and property tax rate, debt, audit findings, recorded votes, contracts, projects and upcoming meetings. Governments
  27. Oct 21, 2026Compare governments side by side. Pick any governments and compare budgets, tax rates, debt and staffing, in total or per resident. Compare
  28. Oct 21, 2026Upcoming public meetings. Every board's next meetings, with agenda links and the money items on each agenda, checked daily. Add them all to your phone or computer calendar with one link. Meetings
  29. Oct 21, 2026Campaign money and contracts. The most recent campaign of each current elected official: how much they raised, from individuals and organizations, and which donors also hold contracts with local governments. Individual donors are counted, not named; each official's full report is linked. Campaign Gifts
  30. Oct 21, 2026Request a public record. Pick a government and a record this site is missing, or describe your own, and get a ready-to-send Florida public records request with the right office's contact details. Records Request
  31. Oct 21, 2026Open data downloads. Every dataset on the site, as CSV and JSON files you can open in a spreadsheet, with a description of each column. Open Data
  32. Oct 21, 2026Zoning and development near your address. My Address now shows the zoning and future land use at the address, development projects and orders within half a mile, recent rezonings, and conditional uses nearby. My Address
  33. Oct 21, 2026BayCounty.me en español. My Address, the records request tool and the glossary are now available in Spanish. Mi dirección, solicitudes de registros y el glosario, en español. En español
  34. Oct 21, 2026Address lookup understands more ways of typing an address. My Address now finds addresses typed with full street types (Drive, Avenue, Boulevard), directions (North, West), highway forms (Hwy 98, US 98, County Road 2321), Saint or St., spelled-out numbers (Tenth Street), MLK, unit numbers, and a city, state and ZIP on the end. If a street type is wrong, it shows the closest matches. My Address
  35. Oct 21, 2026Votes: filters and corrections. Filter recorded votes by government, member, year and outcome, sort by date, amount or closest margin, and share a filtered view by link. Corrections checked against the original minutes: removed two Lynn Haven votes counted twice from a second copy of the same minutes; fixed member votes on a Lynn Haven CRA vote and a Panama City Beach vote that had been misread; restored amounts cut off or misread from scanned pages; and noted fiscal-year and resolution-number typos that appear in the minutes themselves. How They Voted
  36. Oct 21, 2026Clearer vote summaries. Every recorded vote now has a plain-English headline and summary saying what was decided, rewritten from the motion in the minutes. The earlier text was pasted from the minutes and was often hard to read. Each card shows who voted which way and links to the minutes. How They Voted
  37. Oct 21, 2026Correction: flood zones on My Address. Flood zones now come live from FEMA's National Flood Hazard Layer, which reflects Bay County's October 24, 2024 flood maps. The earlier lookup used an older county copy that missed high-risk zones for some addresses (about 8% in a sample of 300) and showed outdated base flood elevations. Results now also show the FIRM panel and any map revision, with a link to check the address on FEMA's map. Thanks to the readers who reported it. My Address
  38. Oct 21, 2026Link previews. Every page now shows a title, summary and image with its headline number when a link is shared in a text, email or social post. Home
  39. Oct 21, 2026Send a correction from the site. A contact form on the About page sends corrections, records and questions straight to the BayCounty.me team. Email is optional and only used to reply. About
  40. Oct 21, 2026Correction: Lynn Haven commission, and a cleaner officials page. Corey Langford is listed as Lynn Haven's fifth commissioner; Judy Tinder's recorded votes remain on the votes page as a former member. The officials page now shows each board as a simple roster, with notes under names and county officers as cards. Springfield and Parker are described as clerk-run cities with no manager, and Parker's City Clerk, Rachelle Stanley, is listed. Officials · How They Voted
  41. Oct 20, 2026How they voted. Recorded votes on budgets, tax rates, contracts, land and borrowing, with every member's vote and split votes first. Now covers the County Commission and every city: 351 votes, 115 of them split. How They Voted
  42. Oct 20, 2026Contracts and vendors. Major contracts approved by Bay County and its cities since October 2022: winners, how they were awarded, change orders, emergency purchases, the bidding rules, and who the county actually paid the most. Contracts
  43. Oct 20, 2026Fees, utility bills and who runs Bay County. Every flat fee on Bay County tax bills, a typical monthly water, sewer and garbage bill in each city, and a guide to every elected seat, appointed manager and top salary. My Address now shows the actual fees on each parcel. Fees & Bills · Officials · My Address
  44. Oct 20, 2026Designed PDF reports, and a new home page. Export PDF now builds a formatted report on every page, with tables, charts, maps and source links. The home page shows headline numbers and every topic. Home · My Address · CRA Spending · Tax Dollars
  45. Oct 20, 2026Tourist tax and sales tax projects. The 5% bed tax: ten years of collections, how the pennies are split and what Visit Panama City Beach spends. Plus a map of 1,062 half-cent sales tax projects with their status, and the school half-cent. Tourist Tax · Sales Tax Projects
  46. Oct 20, 2026Debt and audit findings. What every Bay County government owes, including big single payments ahead, and three years of audit findings in plain English, from audits filed with the Florida Auditor General. Debt · Audit Findings
  47. Oct 20, 2026Search, and Hurricane Michael recovery money. New: search across every page and record. New page tracking FEMA, HUD and state Hurricane Michael money in Bay County, with all 765 FEMA projects. Search · Hurricane Michael
  48. Oct 20, 2026My Address. Look up any Bay County address for a tax estimate, representatives, schools, flood and evacuation zones, and CRA status. Uses the 2026 property roll. My Address · Home
  49. Oct 20, 2026Amendment 3, and the homestead exemption fix. New Amendment 3 page with the state's estimates for every Bay County government. Calculators now use the inflation-adjusted second homestead exemption ($25,722 for FY2026 bills). Amendment 3 · Tax Dollars · Glossary
  50. Oct 20, 2026Tax dollars and the plain-English guide. How your tax dollars are allocated: bill calculator, six years of rates and levies, and every government's budget. Plus a glossary and tap-to-explain terms across the site. Tax Dollars · Glossary · CRA Spending
  51. Oct 20, 2026CRA Spending launched. Map and ledger of Bay County's six Community Redevelopment Agencies. CRA Spending · About
