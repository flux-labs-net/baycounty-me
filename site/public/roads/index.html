The simple version

Who fixes which road

  1. The state runs the highways. State roads such as US 98, US 231, SR 77 and SR 390 belong to the Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT). Its District 3 office in Chipley plans and hires the work, mostly with state fuel tax and federal money.
  2. The county and cities run the rest. Neighborhood streets and county roads are theirs. Much of that paving is paid for by the half-cent infrastructure sales tax voters approved in 2016.
  3. The plan comes first. Each year FDOT adopts a five-year work program: a study, then design, then buying land, then construction. A project's construction year can move as money changes.

Where this comes from

  • State contracts: FDOT's public map of every construction and maintenance contract, read weekly. FDOT leaves some old contracts marked active; a contract counts as under way here only when FDOT's map flags it active, and as finished once its final acceptance or estimated finish date has passed.
  • Five-year plan: FDOT's current adopted work program layers, read weekly: each project's phases and the fiscal years they're funded in.
  • County and city work: the county's half-cent sales tax project map, the same data as the sales tax page.
  • Lane closures change by the hour, so they aren't copied here; FL511 has them live.
