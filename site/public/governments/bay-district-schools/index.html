What the auditors found

5 findings in FY2025 · 9 across 3 audits

Every year outside accountants check the books. "Clean" means the numbers can be trusted overall; "qualified" means the auditor couldn't sign off on part of them. Findings are the to-do list, and a repeat finding means it wasn't fixed.

Bay District Schools · findings by year, oldest to newest: 1 → 3 → 5
  • FY2025 Clean opinion 5 findings Audit report
    1. Audit Adjusting Entries 2025-001 Repeat

      Significant corrections were needed, including unearned state funds not identified, an unrecorded IT subscription agreement, misclassified capital spending, and OPEB, accrual and lease adjustments. The auditor ties it partly to a lack of appropriate staffing.

      Management response: Not corrected; per district, still having significant staff turnover and understaffing.

    2. Expenditures not limited to budgeted amounts (first reported by Auditor General as AM 2023-001) 2025-002 Repeat

      General Fund spending exceeded the budget in three functional categories by a total of $326,700, and the Board did not amend the budget by September 10 as state law and Board policy require. Caused by year-end adjustments not reflected in budget monitoring.

      Management response: Over-expenditures came from year-end reclassifications and accruals; still implementing a detailed year-end budget-to-actual review.

    3. Return of Title IV funds worksheets not documented as independently reviewed (federal, Pell Grant, Haney Technical College) 2025-101

      Refunds of federal student aid were calculated correctly, but there was no sign-off showing an independent review. Material weakness; no questioned costs.

      Management response: Corrective action plan attached to report.

    4. Return of Title IV funds not made within required timeline (federal, Pell Grant) 2025-102

      For 2 of 9 students tested, federal aid was returned to the U.S. Department of Education after the 45-day deadline because of manual-control human error. Could risk future Pell funding. Significant deficiency.

      Management response: Corrective action plan attached to report.

    5. Multi-factor authentication not required for student information (federal, Pell / Gramm-Leach-Bliley) 2025-103

      The district's system did not require multi-factor authentication to access student information, as federal student-information security rules require. Material weakness.

      Management response: Corrective action plan attached to report.

  • FY2024 Clean opinion 3 findings Audit report
    1. Lack of Appropriate Staffing Leading to Audit Entries 2024-001

      Staffing shortfalls meant the district's own processes missed entries the auditors had to make. Material weakness.

      Management response: Repeated as 2025-001.

    2. Wage rate (Davis-Bacon) compliance (federal, COVID-19 Education Stabilization Fund AL 84.425) 2024-002

      Two construction contracts shifted mid-stream to federal pandemic funds lacked the required Davis-Bacon wage clause, and certified payrolls were not collected. Questioned costs: $773,629. Significant deficiency.

      Management response: Reported corrected in FY2025 summary schedule.

    3. Expenditures not limited to budgeted amounts (repeat of AM 2023-001) 2024-003 Repeat

      General Fund spending again exceeded budgeted function amounts without a timely Board amendment.

      Management response: Repeated as 2025-002.

  • FY2023 Clean opinion 1 finding Audit report
    1. Budgetary controls AM 2023-001

      General Fund spending exceeded budgeted amounts in six functional categories by $9,532 to $218,198 (total $582,437) and the Board did not amend the budget by September 10.

      Management response: Overages came from year-end reclassifications and accruals; district will add a detailed year-end budget-to-actual review.

State auditor reports

Florida Auditor General reviews beyond the yearly audit.

Operational Audit (FY2022-23, follow-up on Report No. 2021-121) (Report No. 2024-088, December 2023)
  • Finding 1. Required level 2 background screenings were not always performed for contractor workers on school grounds (repeat of a 2021 finding); the district kept no complete list of such workers.
  • Finding 2. Volunteers sometimes started work before the district verified their backgrounds, contrary to state law and Board policy.
  • Finding 3. Students did not always receive the required youth mental health awareness instruction.
  • Finding 4. Controls over construction management entity (CME) subcontractor services and payments still needed improvement.
  • Finding 5. Records did not show the district monitored whether CME 'general conditions' costs were reasonable (repeat of a 2021 finding).
