What the auditors found

7 findings in FY2025 · 23 across 3 audits

Every year outside accountants check the books. "Clean" means the numbers can be trusted overall; "qualified" means the auditor couldn't sign off on part of them. Findings are the to-do list, and a repeat finding means it wasn't fixed.

Mexico Beach · findings by year, oldest to newest: 8 → 8 → 7
  • FY2025 Qualified opinion 7 findings Audit report
    1. Management Override and Segregation of Duties (sixth-year repeat) 2025-001 Repeat

      The finance office is too small, and had turnover, so the same person could prepare and approve adjusting journal entries. Errors or misstatements could go undetected.

      Management response: New policies created in FY25; Finance Director to add procedures involving outside accounting advisors. Target Mar 31, 2027.

    2. Budgetary Control 2025-002 Repeat

      No budget was legally adopted for the Disaster Recovery Fund, even though state law requires spending to follow an adopted budget.

      Management response: Finance Director and City Administrator will adopt budgets for all funds. Target Jul 22, 2026.

    3. Impact Fee Accounting 2025-003 Repeat

      Impact fees were not kept in a separate fund, as s. 163.31801 F.S. requires.

      Management response: Will create a separate impact fee fund. Target Jul 22, 2026.

    4. Building Permit Expenditures 2025-004 Repeat

      Building department spending is not budgeted or tracked separately, so the city cannot show it complies with the state cap on carrying forward building permit fees (s. 553.80(7)(a) F.S.).

      Management response: Will budget and track all building department expenses. Target Oct 31, 2026.

    5. Debt Compliance 2025-005 Repeat

      The bond sinking fund was underfunded at year end, which breaks the bond covenants.

      Management response: Monthly transfers to the sinking funds have been adjusted to meet covenants. Immediate.

    6. Deficit in Unrestricted Net Position 2025-006 Repeat

      The utility (enterprise) funds ended the year with a deficit in unrestricted net position, meaning liabilities exceed the assets available for general use. The auditor suggests rate changes, cost control or better long-term planning.

      Management response: First phase of a Florida Rural Water rate study done; city will implement its recommendations. Target Dec 31, 2026.

    7. Compliance with State Reporting Requirements 2025-007

      The city missed the Nov. 30 deadline for the annual Public Depositor Report to the state CFO (risking state protection of public deposits), and an elected official filed the annual financial disclosure with the Commission on Ethics late.

      Management response: Finance Director was not aware of the deadlines; now designated primary contact for both filings. Immediate.

State auditor reports

Florida Auditor General reviews beyond the yearly audit.

Operational Audit (period Oct. 2022 - Jan. 2024) (Report No. 2025-095, January 2025)
  • Finding 1. Heavy turnover: from Jan. 2020 to Aug. 2024 the City Administrator position turned over 3 times, City Clerk 2 times, and City Accountant 3 times (vacant 495 days). Records showed allegations of a negative workplace and conflicts between management and elected officials.
  • Finding 2. Audits and annual financial reports were filed late for FY2020-21 through FY2022-23 (FY2021-22 audit was 362 days late). The Department of Revenue withheld $37,137 ($29,166 half-cent sales tax + $7,971 municipal revenue sharing) as of August 2024.
  • Finding 3. Competitive procurement not followed: 5 purchases totaling $564,092 (including $250,000 disaster debris removal, $130,772 fuel from the same local vendor for at least 19 years, $110,260 yard waste) were not competitively selected; other bids/RFPs lacked advertising documentation.
  • Finding 4. 9 purchases totaling $1 million lacked City Council approval required for purchases over $10,000; purchase orders were not used as required, and receipt of goods/services was not always documented.
  • Finding 5. The city paid a contractor the same $450,031 invoice twice; the contractor refunded $328,964 on Dec. 13, 2023 (the duplicate less $121,067 retainage).
  • Finding 6. The city kept paying its contracted City Accountant after the agreement expired (April 2021) and issued 1099s instead of W-2s without documenting why she was treated as an independent contractor.
  • Finding 7. Application security: user access privileges were not limited to what was necessary.
  • Finding 8. IT access was not always promptly removed when no longer needed.
  • Finding 9. No policies for reporting, investigating and communicating known or suspected fraud.
