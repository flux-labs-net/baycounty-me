Taxes

The bed tax: what visitors pay and where it goes

Visitors who rent a hotel room, condo or house for six months or less pay a 5% tourist development tax on top of sales tax. It brings in tens of millions a year, almost all from Panama City Beach, and state law limits what it can be spent on.

The simple version

How the bed tax works

  1. Visitors pay it, not residents. It's added to short-term rentals in a taxing area covering Panama City Beach, Panama City, Mexico Beach and nearby areas.
  2. Each penny has a job. State law ties the pennies to tourism marketing, beach nourishment, and tourist facilities like sports complexes.
  3. A council recommends, the County Commission decides. The 9-member Tourist Development Council (TDC) recommends spending; the County Commission adopts the budget. Visit Panama City Beach (the CVB) runs most of the PCB-zone money under a county contract.
  • 2% Original tourist development tax since 1986
  • 1% Additional 1 percent tax since 1997
  • 1% Professional sports franchise facility tax since 2009
  • 1% Additional professional sports franchise facility tax since 2009

State law would allow a 6th cent. Bay County doesn't levy it; the County Commission declined to add it in 2023.

    Who decides

    • Statutory composition. s.125.0104(4)(e) F.S.: the county governing board appoints a 9-member Tourist Development Council established by ordinance: the county commission chair (or a commissioner the chair designates); two elected municipal officials, at least one from the most populous municipality in the taxing district; six people involved in the tourist industry, of whom 3 to 4 must be owners/operators of tourist accommodations subject to the tax. All members must be county electors; staggered 4-year terms; meets at least quarterly; recommends uses of revenue and continuously reviews expenditures. The council is advisory; the Board of County Commissioners adopts the budget. Source
    • Bay County TDC as described by Visit PCB. Nine members appointed by the Bay County Board of County Commissioners: three tax collectors, three representatives of tourist-related businesses and three elected officials. The TDC contracts marketing and promotional activities with the Panama City Beach CVB. Source
    • Budget flow. The TDC approves and recommends the PCB CVB, Destination Panama City and Mexico Beach budgets/programs of work to the BCC (Aug 25, 2026 agenda for FY2027). The BCC adopts TDC Funds 124-129 in the county budget. Source
    • Sixth penny requires referendum (as reported). In June 2023 the TDC asked the BCC to raise the rate from 5% to 6%; the motion died for lack of a second. The article reports that a state law change effective July 1, 2023 requires voter approval for future increases. Source
    • Finance controls (2026). On Jan 13, 2026 the TDC approved Resolution 2601 giving the Bay County Clerk of Court control of the CVB's bank accounts and financial operations, and hired an independent auditor to review internal controls. Source
    • Officers reported in March 2026. Reggie Johns replaced Clair Pease as chair; Ethan Register vice chair; Will Fleming and Josh Wakstein joined the board. Jeff DeBenedictis became treasurer in January 2026. This is a partial list from news reports, not an official roster. Source
    • Executive. Griff Griffitts (then a state representative) was selected as Visit PCB president & CEO on March 10, 2026, after Dan Rowe resigned; BCC was to finalize the hire. Source

    Recent decisions and news

    1. Jun 20, 2023Sixth penny rejected. The BCC did not raise the TDT from 5% to 6% after a TDC request; the motion died for lack of a second. Officials from Panama City Beach, Panama City and Mexico Beach backed the increase; some residents opposed it. Source
    2. Sep 4, 2024Beach nourishment money opened to public safety. The BCC approved letting up to 10% of the beach-renourishment cent go to public safety (sheriff, PCB police, water safety, lifeguards). About $50 million in unused renourishment money was placed in reserves for emergencies. Visit PCB's share rose from 8% to 10% (annual budget $2.6M to $3.2M, per WMBB). Reporting figures do not fully reconcile. Source
    3. 2025State legislation. 2025 House proposals (HB 1221 amendment, HB 7033) would have redirected bed-tax revenue to property-tax relief or required periodic renewal; local tourism groups opposed them. Source
    4. 2026-01CVB credit card fraud charges. Two Visit PCB vice presidents were arrested on grand theft and fraudulent credit-card-use charges (allegations, not convictions). Investigators allege nearly $1 million in charges on one executive's company card from April 2024 to January 2026, of which about $58,000 was documented as business expense. The Clerk took over CVB finances. Source
    5. Jan 30, 2026CEO resignation. Dan Rowe, CVB/TDC president and CEO for 18 years, resigned after being on paid leave since January 13. The TDC accepted the resignation on Feb 2, 2026. No reason was given in the article. Source
    6. Mar 10, 2026New CEO. The TDC selected State Rep. Griff Griffitts as president & CEO. Some residents raised conflict-of-interest concerns about his continued legislative service; he said the TDC/CVB do not request state appropriations. Source
    7. Jun 9, 2026SABRE event center paused. The TDC paused design of the SABRE Event Center / Western Regional Resiliency Center at Publix Sports Park, a facility WMBB described as $60 million, after an outside consultant recommended against the site. About $1.2M had been spent on design. The CVB FY2026 budget had $26,284,629 for the project. Source
    8. Aug 25, 2026FY2027 plan. The TDC approved a FY2027 plan reported at more than $54 million. Source
    9. Oct 1, 2026Collection agency change. TDT administration moved from the Clerk of Court to the Bay County Tax Collector. Source

    Criminal charges described above are allegations, not convictions.
