What Bay County governments owe

Local governments borrow to build things now and pay over time. Here's what each one owes, what for, and what it pays each year, from their audited financial statements.

From audits filed with the Florida Auditor General
Data updated Oct 21, 2026
The simple version

How local government borrowing works

  1. Borrow now, repay later. A bond or loan pays for something big today, like a water plant or a building, and gets paid back with interest over years.
  2. Each debt names its money source. Most local debt here is repaid from a specific stream (sales tax, utility bills, tourist tax) rather than from a property tax voters approved. That's why almost none of it is "general obligation" debt.
  3. Most of it is bank loans. In Bay County, nearly all borrowing is placed directly with banks or the state, not sold to the public as bonds.
  4. Pensions are a separate promise. Money owed to employees' future pensions and retiree health care is listed separately; it isn't borrowing, but it's still owed.

Gaps in the bond records (13)
  • City of Callaway: FEMA Community Disaster Loan not listed as an issue (forgiven per the audit; original amount 2250000).
  • City of Springfield: FEMA Community Disaster Loan not listed as an issue (forgiven per the audit; original amount 2902896).
  • Panama City-Bay County Airport and Industrial District: State Infrastructure Bank Loan (agreement dated 2024-06-27) not listed as an issue (no balance drawn/outstanding at the audit date per the audit; original amount 26000000).
  • EMMA (emma.msrb.org): its public search-ahead endpoint answered and returned issuer pages for Bay County, Panama City, Panama City Beach, Lynn Haven and Callaway (listed in emma_issuer_urls), but issuer and document pages sit behind EMMA's Terms of Use, which prohibit scraping, robots and automated downloading. Official statements and continuing-disclosure filings on EMMA were therefore not opened; official_statement_url is empty for every issue. Someone should open the EMMA issuer pages by hand to attach OS links, CUSIPs and current ratings.
  • No official statement for any Bay County government was found posted on the issuers' own websites (Bay County, Clerk of Court, Panama City, Panama City Beach, Callaway, Lynn Haven, Bay District Schools). Panama City Beach posts draft authorizing resolutions for its Series 2020 bonds; those are cited.
  • Florida Division of Bond Finance: since January 2026 local bond filings (Form DBF 2025-1.009, which includes the final official statement) go into bondissue.sbafla.com, which requires a login. No public search was available.
  • Most Bay County government debt is privately placed with banks (notes and direct-placement bonds). These have no official statement and usually no rating. Publicly offered issues identified: Bay County Water & Sewer 2015 and Water 2016; Panama City Beach CIR Series 2020; Callaway 2015; probably Lynn Haven 2015/2016 and School Board COPs 2020A/2022A. Placement type is left blank where the audit did not make it clear.
  • Ratings: the only ratings stated in an official document are Panama City Beach's (S&P 'A' for the 2020 Front Beach Road bonds; S&P AA-/Fitch AA+ for Utility System bonds, per the FY2025 audit). No other rating could be confirmed.
  • Par amounts missing: Bay District Schools COP Series 2015, 2019 (only a not-to-exceed amount) and 2020A; Lynn Haven 2015, 2016 and 2019 bonds; Mexico Beach 2000 and 2013 bonds; several Port Authority loans. The audits give only balances for these.
  • Dated dates are mostly the issue/closing dates the audits give ('On <date> the County issued...'); they may differ slightly from the 'dated as of' date in the bond documents. Dated dates are missing for Lynn Haven, Mexico Beach, Springfield, Parker, Port Authority and several School Board and Panama City notes.
  • Panama City's $150 million Capital Improvement Revenue Note, Series 2023 (Truist) had to mature by 2026-09-01. No FY2025 or FY2026 Panama City audit was available to confirm whether it was repaid or refinanced.
  • Bay County FY2025 and Panama City FY2025 audits were not yet on the Auditor General site, so balances are as of 2024-09-30 for those governments. New debt issued after each audit date is not included.
  • Not covered: Community Development Districts and other independent special districts in Bay County that issue public bonds (e.g., CDDs in the Panama City Beach and West Bay areas), and conduit bonds issued in Bay County's name for private borrowers (e.g., charter school or hospital educational/industrial development revenue bonds). Bay County governments do not repay conduit bonds, and EMMA would be the main source for both.

Sources and gaps

All debt figures come from the audited financial statements filed with the Florida Auditor General. Total debt means bonds, notes, loans, leases, subscription IT arrangements (SBITAs) and finance purchases (plus unamortized bond premium where reported). It excludes compensated absences, claims, landfill closure costs, net pension and OPEB liabilities, which are shown separately.

Latest audit used: FY2025 for Lynn Haven, Callaway, Springfield, Parker, Mexico Beach, Panama City Beach, PCB CRA and the airport district; FY2025 (June 30) for Bay District Schools; FY2024 for Bay County, Panama City, Panama City CRA and the Port Authority, because their FY2025 audits were not on the Auditor General's site as of 2026-10-20.

Almost all local debt here is privately placed with banks or borrowed from the State (State Revolving Fund, State Infrastructure Bank), not publicly sold bonds, so most issues have no credit rating or EMMA listing.

Big near-term balloon payments: Panama City's $150,000,000 Series 2023 note (5.82%) is scheduled to be repaid in one payment in FY2026. Bay County's Hurricane Michael notes are due in one payment each: $49,943,109 on 9/1/2027 and $48,874,337 on 9/1/2028.

Debt service 'year' is the first scheduled year in each audit's maturity table (the year after the audit), unless otherwise noted.

No government reviewed met any s. 218.503(1) financial emergency condition in the audits reviewed. No auditor issued a deteriorating-financial-condition notice. Mexico Beach's FY2022 audit noted late vendor payments tied to Hurricane Michael grant timing, which the auditor said was not a financial emergency.

Only Mexico Beach received a modified (qualified) opinion. The cause, in FY2023-FY2025, was that the city had not implemented GASB 75 (retiree health/OPEB).

What we couldn't find (9)
  • Bay County, City of Panama City, Panama City CRA and Panama City Port Authority FY2025 audits were not filed on the Auditor General site as of 2026-10-20, so FY2024 is used. The Port Authority has only its FY2024 audit posted there.
  • Credit ratings: none of the audits publish ratings, and Moody's/S&P pages could not be read. The only publicly offered bonds found are Bay County's Water & Sewer 2015 and Water 2016 bonds, Callaway 2015, Lynn Haven 2015/2016, and probably the school COPs. Their ratings still need to be checked in the official statements.
  • EMMA (emma.msrb.org), the national bond disclosure site, couldn't be read by our tools, so bond documents there are not linked yet.
  • Original issue amounts are missing for several issues (Mexico Beach, Lynn Haven bonds, Port loans), and per-series COP balances are missing for the school district, because the audits do not list them.
  • Purposes are not stated in the audits for Panama City's $150M Series 2023 note (beyond 'Capital Projects Fund'), the airport's SIB loans, and most Port loans. Council and board minutes or loan resolutions would be needed.
  • Management responses: several reports attach corrective action plans as scanned images (Panama City CRA, PCB 2024, Bay DSB 2025, some Bay County items). Where the text couldn't be read, the page says so.
  • CRAs for Callaway, Springfield and Parker file separate reports. They were not reviewed because the city audits show no CRA debt. Springfield's and Parker's own audits report late or missing city TIF payments to those CRAs.
  • FY2022 audits were reviewed only where needed to complete 3 years (Panama City, Panama City CRA, Bay County). Prior-year findings for the other governments are summarized from the later audits' prior-finding schedules, with brief plain-English text.
