The half-cent sales taxes: what was promised, what got built

Bay County has two extra half-cent sales taxes that voters approved: one for roads and infrastructure, one for school buildings. Here is every infrastructure project on the county's map, and what we could find about the school tax.

Data updated Oct 20, 2026
The simple version

How the half-cent taxes work

  1. Everyone who shops here pays. Each adds half a cent to every dollar of taxable purchases in Bay County, including purchases by tourists.
  2. Voters set the rules. The infrastructure tax started January 2017 and voters extended it in November 2024 through 2036. The school tax runs through 2030.
  3. It can only build things. The infrastructure money goes to roads, bridges, dirt-road paving, drainage and safety projects. The school money goes to school construction, renovation, security and technology.
  4. The county and cities split the infrastructure tax by a state formula based on population.

      Gaps

      • Countywide infrastructure-surtax collections (county + cities) are not published separately. EDR's series combines the infrastructure and school surtaxes.
      • No Citizens Oversight Committee reports or minutes for the infrastructure surtax were found online, and no OPPAGA/independent performance audit for Bay County's 2024 referendum appears on OPPAGA's contracted-reviews page.
      • City-level surtax spending (Panama City, Lynn Haven, PCB, etc.) was not compiled. City project status comes only from the county GIS layer.
      • Bay District Schools' half-cent page and budget PDFs block automated access. The promised 2018 school project list and per-project school surtax costs were not retrieved. Few school projects could be tied to surtax funding by a source.
      • School 'Local sales taxes' for FY2016-FY2017 come from the FY2017 Auditor General audit, and FY2018-FY2025 from the district financial statements filed with the Auditor General. No FY2026 figure is available yet.
