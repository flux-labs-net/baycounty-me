Fees on your tax bill, and what water and garbage cost

Beyond property tax, your tax bill can carry flat fees for fire service, stormwater and lighting. Your utility bill adds water, sewer and garbage. Here's what each government charges.

Data updated Oct 20, 2026
The simple version

Taxes vs. fees

  1. Property tax depends on your home's value. A bigger taxable value means a bigger tax.
  2. Fees on the tax bill are mostly flat. Non-ad valorem assessments charge per home, per parcel or by building size for a specific service, like fire protection or stormwater drainage. They're not affected by homestead exemptions or Amendment 3.
  3. Utility bills are separate. Water, sewer and garbage are billed monthly by your city or the county, based on how much you use.

Bills are worked out from each provider's published rate sheet. Panama City Beach's garbage rate and Parker's utility rates weren't found online. Panama City's own notice gives an "average" bill equal to its base charges alone, so its usage charges may include an allowance the notice doesn't describe.

What we couldn't find

  • Final adopted rate resolutions not located for Panama City fire (commercial per-square-foot rates), Panama City Beach fire/stormwater/CRA (only the maximum-rate notice found), Lynn Haven stormwater (current per-unit rate), and Springfield fire (rate method).
  • Several levy codes could not be matched to a named district: SB, PW, WMH, TWIN, ALSL1, BOCA, OERR, PCN; PPCD (probably Pier Park CDD) and LPWH1 (probably Lake Powell Residential Golf CDD) are inferred.
  • Typical single-family amounts for value-based assessments (PCB fire/stormwater, Lynn Haven stormwater, Springfield fire) can't be stated without parcel-level property-use data. The DOR NAL parcel file for 2025 wasn't available from the portal, so only all-parcel medians are given.
  • Parker: no published water/sewer/garbage rate schedule found, and no FTE count.
  • Panama City Beach: no residential garbage rate and no employee count (the budget book couldn't be downloaded).
  • Panama City: the 5,000-gallon bill is provisional, because the city's 'average bill' equals its base charges alone. No stormwater fee found.
  • Lynn Haven: the FY2026-27 budget (which may carry rates effective Oct 1, 2026) could not be downloaded because the connection was reset; FY2025-26 rates are used.
  • Callaway: the rates page shows no effective date, and the FY2027 budget assumes small consumption increases that may not be on the page yet.
